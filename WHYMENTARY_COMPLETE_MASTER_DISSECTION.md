# 1. Core Philosophy: How Script Dictates Visuals

Whymentary's visual engine operates on a strict **3-Tier Semantic Translation Hierarchy**:

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    SCRIPT-TO-VISUAL HIERARCHY                                          |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------+------------------------------------------------+
| TIER              | SCRIPT CONTEXT                    | VISUAL EXECUTION                              |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------+------------------------------------------------+
| Tier 1: Literal   | Everyday relatable premise / hook | Stickman in everyday situations, literal props |
| Tier 2: Metaphor  | Complex biological/physics concept| Internal machinery (gears/engines), gauges, HUD|
| Tier 3: Abstract  | Molecular/cellular/evolutionary   | Cross-sections, particles, chemical pathways   |
+-------------------+-----------------------------------+------------------------------------------------+
```

### The 4 Principles of Script-to-Visual Synchronicity:
1. **Additive Stacking (Clause-by-Clause Pop-Ins):** Visual elements do not appear all at once. An empty white canvas receives elements progressively as the voiceover mentions each noun:
   * *Voiceover:* `"If you are sitting in a room..."` $\rightarrow$ Stickman sits down.
   * *Voiceover:* `"...you probably have a fan running."` $\rightarrow$ Fan appears on table.
   * *Voiceover:* `"Maybe you even have two."` $\rightarrow$ Second fan slides in from frame right.
2. **Visual Puns & Comedic Contrast:** The voiceover maintains an authoritative, calm documentary tone, while the visual character delivers comedic slapstick (e.g. sweating buckets, head exploding with question marks, jumping ecstatically on cash sacks).
3. **The "Mechanical Analogy" Translation:** Abstract scientific concepts are never explained with dry textbook jargon alone; they are always materialized as physical contraptions:
   * *Thermoregulation* $\rightarrow$ Combustion car engine with cooling fan inside the torso.
   * *Dopamine Adaptation* $\rightarrow$ A dangling carrot / conveyor belt treadmill.
   * *Immune Defense* $\rightarrow$ Green acid pool zapping bacteria with electric sizzles.
4. **Color-Coded Semantic Anchor:** Colors are assigned permanent conceptual roles:
   * **Red (`#E51919`):** Heat, danger, failure, high acidity, bitten skin, angry emotions.
   * **Green (`#7ED957`):** Plant biology, nutrients, gastric acid, money.
   * **Blue (`#2F54EB`):** Cool airflow, water, low temperatures, baseline states.
   * **Pink (`#F8C8DC`):** Organic organs, stomach lining, human flesh.

### Character Model Sheet (The "Stickman"):
* **Head:** Perfect circle with smooth black contour.
  * *Eyes:* Two solid black dots (`#000000`).
  * *Eyebrows:* Simple arched lines that tilt upward for confusion/worry, downward for anger.
  * *Mouth Variations:* Simple curved line (smile/frown) or open oval cutout with flat red fill (`#E51919`) representing the tongue/oral cavity.

* **Limbs:** Single-stroke straight lines with defined 45° to 90° angular joint bends (elbows, knees).
* **Hands / Feet:** Hands are simplified 3-finger trident strokes or closed round circles; feet are short horizontal heel-to-toe strokes.

# 3. The Layered Pop-In Animation Engine & Micro-Motion Mechanics

Whymentary does **NOT** use conventional hard video cuts for pacing. The visual engagement is driven by a **Continuous Layered Pop-In & Micro-Animation Engine**.

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    THE 6-LAYER STACKING ARCHITECTURE                                   |
+---------+--------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| LAYER   | NAME               | FUNCTION & BEHAVIOR                                                     |
+---------+--------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Layer 5 | Typography & UI    | Big block marker text, question marks, warning symbols ("X", "!!!").   |
| Layer 4 | Interactive Props  | Fans, tables, thermometers, money bags, cars, trees, meat chunks.       |
| Layer 3 | Facial/Body FX     | Sweat droplets (cyan), mouth swaps, blinking eyes, shaking limbs.      |
| Layer 2 | Stickman Base      | Character skeleton / pose (sitting, standing, running, lying down).    |
| Layer 1 | Ambient Dynamics   | Continuous looping motion: wiggly heat waves, wind gusts, fire sparks.  |
| Layer 0 | Canvas & Floor     | Solid pure white background (#FFFFFF) with optional horizontal baseline.|
+---------+--------------------+-------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### The Pop-In Easing Specification:
When a new asset enters the screen (a fan, a thermometer, an exclamation mark, a text callout), it enters using an **Elastic Overshoot Easing**:
* **Duration:** 6 frames (~0.25 seconds at 24fps).
* **Keyframes:**
  * Frame 0: `Scale = 0%`, `Opacity = 100%`
  * Frame 4: `Scale = 112%` (Overshoot peak)
  * Frame 6: `Scale = 100%` (Settled state)
* **Auditory Sync:** Accompanied by a crisp, subtle "pop", "click", or "swoosh" Foley sound effect synchronized exactly with the spoken keyword.

### Micro-Animation & Continuous Motion (Preventing Dead Frames):
Even when no new props pop in, the canvas is never frozen. Micro-animations maintain visual energy:
1. **Wavy Line Ripples (2-3 Frame Cycles):** Heat waves, wind currents, and steam use an alternating 2-frame or 3-frame path deformation cycle to create a shimmer effect.
2. **Sweat Particle Spawning:** Blue sweat droplets pop onto the stickman's head and drop downward before fading out.
3. **Character Pose Swaps:** The stickman's arms, mouth, and head angle swap instantaneously on punchlines (e.g. relaxed smile $\rightarrow$ hands on head with open screaming mouth).
4. **Prop Internal Motion:** Fan blades rotate continuously at 24fps (blur disk or rotating spokes); gauge needles tremble/oscillate before pinning into the red zone.

# 5. Channel Consistency Rules & Production Checklist

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    MASTER PRODUCTION CHECKLIST                                         |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| [ ] Canvas is 100% pure white (#FFFFFF) with zero textures, paper patterns, or dark mode gradients.   |

| [ ] On-screen text is hand-lettered uppercase marker script with underlined emphasis on keywords.     |
| [ ] Voiceover delivery is warm, conversational, and authoritative, punctuated by crisp Foley clicks.   |
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

# 6. The 7 "Make-or-Break" Elements of Whymentary

If ANY of these 7 core items are missing or miscalculated, the video will fail to replicate Whymentary's viral retention and engagement:

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    THE 7 MAKE-OR-BREAK PILLARS                                         |
+---+-----------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| # | PILLAR                      | THE MAKE-OR-BREAK STANDARD                                           |
+---+-----------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1 | The 5-Second Inverted Hook  | Must open with a relatable everyday observation immediately inverted |
|   | (Scripting)                 | by a shocking paradox (e.g., "It's hot -> your fan is killing you").  |
+---+-----------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2 | Mechanical Analogy Rule     | NEVER explain science with abstract text or graphs. Materialize it   |
|   | (Visual Translation)        | as a physical contraption (car engine in chest, acid flask, gears).  |
+---+-----------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 3 | Clause-by-Clause Pop-Ins    | Do NOT show full scenes at once. Elements must stack incrementally    |
|   | (Visual Pacing)             | on the white canvas as each noun/verb is spoken (1 beat / 1.9-2.5s).  |
+---+-----------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 4 | Elastic Overshoot Easing    | Every pop-in MUST use a 6-frame overshoot curve (0% -> 112% -> 100%).|
|   | (Animation Engine)          | Linear or abrupt transitions make the production look amateur.       |
+---+-----------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 5 | Continuous Micro-Motion     | Canvas must never be frozen. Looping 2-frame heat waves, wind gusts, |
|   | (Alive Canvas)              | sweat bubbles, and eye blinks prevent visual fatigue.                |
+---+-----------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 6 | Deadpan vs Slapstick Tone   | Authoritative, calm documentary voiceover juxtaposed against comic   |
|   | (Audio-Visual Contrast)     | stickman suffering, panic, and exaggerated slapstick reactions.      |
+---+-----------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
| 7 | Tactile Foley Sound Design  | Every visual pop-in, cross-out, and gauge swing MUST have a crisp    |
|   | (Audio Immersion)           | acoustic trigger (bubble pop, wooden click, swoosh, bell ding).      |
+---+-----------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------------+
```
